# Lesson 3 — Creating a Profile

This lesson is about one thing: creating a record for somebody who does not have one yet, and creating it so that the next person can find them.

---

## Opening

**[This is a Lashes Animated Block]**

Last lesson you found somebody who was already there.

Desmond had a record. Most people do.

Even Lefty had a record. It was just filed under a name he does not go by.

This lesson is the other one.

You searched. You searched properly. Fragments, the year, the alternate spelling, the last four. You asked what else they have been called and you searched that too.

And nothing came back, because there is no record of her yet.

So now you make the record.

I need you to understand what that actually is, because it is not a form.

## Nobody's Record

**[Text block]**

Later that same week. A woman is sitting on a low wall near the corner where your team has been working. She has been there a few days.

She tells you her name is Bobbi.

You search. You do the whole thing, the way you have practiced. The fragment. The year. Bobbie with an ie, Bobby with a y. You try the last name once she gives it, and the first three letters of it on their own.

Nothing comes back.

For most people, an empty result means you have not found them yet. That was the whole point of the last lesson. But sometimes it means exactly what it looks like.

**[Statement block]**

> Bobbi has a history. HMIS does not have it yet.
> That is a gap in the record, not a gap in her.

**[Text block]**

Think about what that gap costs her.

There is no record of where she has been staying. No note from anybody who has spoken with her before. Nothing that documents how long she has been experiencing homelessness, which is the single thing most housing programs need to establish.

If she walks into a shelter tonight, or an outreach worker meets her three months from now on a different block, they will start from nothing. The same way you just did.

That changes in the next few minutes.

What it changes into is up to you.

## Create, or Keep Looking?

**[Text block]**

Three search histories. Each one is what you have done so far for a person standing in front of you. For each, decide: create the record now, or keep looking.

Nothing is scored. You will be told why either way.

**[Custom Code Block: three scenario cards, each with two buttons, Create and Keep looking]**

**Scenario 1**

You searched *Marcus*. Nothing came back.

[Choice: Create] **Not yet.** One search is not due diligence. A first name alone is the weakest search there is, and an empty result from it tells you almost nothing. Try the last name, a fragment, the year of birth. Ask what else he has been called.

[Choice: Keep looking] **Right.** One search proves nothing. You have not tried the last name, a fragment, a year, or an alias. Last lesson you found Lefty on *Tor* after *Lefty* came back empty.

**Scenario 2**

You searched the last name, then the first three letters of it, then the year of birth, then the last four of her Social Security Number. You asked what else she has been called and searched those too. Two near matches came back. You opened both and checked household, program history, and location. Neither fits the woman in front of you.

[Choice: Create] **Right.** That is what due diligence looks like. Every identifier, every alias, both near misses opened and checked against the person. Create the record, and send the Unique Identifier to HMIS Support afterward so they can double-check.

[Choice: Keep looking] **You can stop.** There is nothing left to try that you have not tried. Searching past this point does not protect her from a duplicate. It just delays her record. Create it, and send the Unique Identifier to HMIS Support so they can look across agencies.

**Scenario 3**

You searched the name and date of birth. One result: same last name, same first initial, born the same year. No Social Security Number on file, no photo, no household.

[Choice: Keep looking] **Right.** A name and a year are not enough to say it is her, and not enough to say it is not. Open the record and check the rest of it: programs, location, case notes. Last lesson you told two David Nguyens apart on the Location tab.

## The Codes, From the Other Side

**[Text block]**

You have met these before.

Last lesson they were something you read. They sat next to the name and the date of birth and the Social Security Number, and they were how you told a record that was thin from a record that was wrong.

Now you are the one setting them.

They do not describe the person. They describe the answer.

| Field | Its code |
|---|---|
| Social Security Number | Quality of SSN |
| First name and Last name | Quality of Name |
| Date of Birth | Quality of DOB |

Each one has the same five options: full, partial, client doesn't know, client prefers not to answer, and data not collected.

Three of those look interchangeable. They are not.

**Data not collected** means you did not ask.
**Client doesn't know** means you asked, and they could not tell you.
**Client prefers not to answer** means you asked, and they decided not to.

Those are three different conversations, and the next worker will act on each of them differently. Someone who declined does not need to be asked again. Someone who could not remember might be able to bring a document next time. Someone who was never asked has simply not been asked.

LAHSA's data quality guide says it in its own words: incomplete responses will keep showing on the dashboard as data quality issues, and they are appropriate if accurate.

## When There Is No Answer to Put in the Box

**[Text block]**

The code tells the truth about the answer. The field beside it still has to hold something, because it is required and the form will not save without it.

LAHSA has a convention for each one. None of them is a guess. Each is a placeholder that everyone in the system recognizes as a placeholder, and the code beside it is what says so.

**[Tabs, 3]**

**Name**

Use the name the participant goes by. If that is not the name on their ID, the name they identify with is the one that goes in the fields, unless a funder specifically requires the legal name. Quality of Name: Full name reported. The other name goes in Alias.

If they give you a street name or a code name, or in outreach you are recording a modified name for their safety, that goes in the name fields too. Quality of Name: Partial, street name, or code name reported.

If you cannot get any name from them, LAHSA's guidance is a brief description in the name fields, so the next worker can recognize who the record belongs to. Neutral, observable, and nothing you would not say in front of them. Quality of Name: Partial, street name, or code name reported.

If they are unable to give a name, or prefer not to, the code is Client doesn't know or Client prefers not to answer.

One more reason the name matters: it is the name that goes on the Universal Housing Application.

**Date of Birth**

If they know the year and not the day: enter January 1 and the year. 01/01/1974. Quality of DOB: Approximate or partial DOB reported.

If they do not know the year either: ask their age. Subtract it from the current year. Enter January 1 and that year. Same code.

If they truly cannot or will not give you a date or an age, the code is Client doesn't know or Client prefers not to answer.

Date of Birth is a high priority field. It is how the system determines age, and age decides eligibility for a great deal.

**Social Security Number**

Type an X for every digit you do not have. Some digits: XXX-XX-6789. Quality of SSN: Approximate or partial SSN reported.

If the participant does not have a Social Security Number at all: XXX-XX-XXXX, and Quality of SSN: Client doesn't know.

If they have one and decline to share it: XXX-XX-XXXX, and Quality of SSN: Client prefers not to answer.

**[Callout]**

The line between a placeholder and a guess is whether the code beside it tells the truth. LAHSA's data standards prohibit purposely recording inaccurate information, and that is what a guess is. A placeholder with an honest code is not a guess. It is the record saying, plainly, what it does not know yet.

Client doesn't know and Client prefers not to answer show up on the dashboard as warnings: somebody reviews them, and if they are accurate, they stay. Data not collected shows up as an error, because it means a required question was never asked. It stays an error until somebody asks.

## Permission to Share

**[Text block]**

Now the second question.

You asked at the start whether you could write things down. This is different: may other agencies that use HMIS see what you wrote down.

A yes here is what lets a shelter worker across town find her record tonight. It is what the whole system runs on, and it is entirely her decision.

Ask it plainly:

> "The information I write down, other agencies that use this system can see it too. A shelter, an outreach team on another block. That is what lets them pick up where we left off instead of starting over. Is that okay with you?"

The form calls this the Release of Information. The paper version is **Form 1125, HMIS Intake and Enrollment**, and the part she is agreeing to is the **Consent to Share Protected Personal Information**. It is valid for seven years, and that is written into the form itself.

**[Tabs, 5]**

**Electronic Signature**

The recommended path whenever you have a device. Pick it, and the full text of the consent form appears on the screen. Scroll through it with her. At the bottom: a checkbox for consent to share her photograph, which is its own question, then two signature lines, **Client Signature** and **Organization Staff Signature**. On a tablet or touchscreen, she signs with a finger or a pen. Then you sign.

**Attached PDF**

You already have the signed form as a file. A **File** field appears. Drag it in or choose it. Images, PDFs, and Word documents, up to 25 MB.

**Signed Paper Document**

The signed paper exists, and it is not being uploaded right now. A **Location** field appears. Record where the paper copy is kept, so the next worker can find it.

**Verbal Consent**

She said yes, and there is nothing to sign on. Record Verbal Consent. This one comes with an obligation. Verbal consent is real consent, and it is not permanent documentation. **You must plan to follow up for written consent.** This will be the one you use most in the field.

**Household**

For a minor in a household, consent is tracked through the Head of Household. Households are their own lesson. This is the one line you need today.

**[Callout]**

**If the answer is No.** Mark No. Still create the profile. Her record exists, with her real details in it, and your agency can see all of it. Other agencies cannot see the identifying information. She has not left the system. She has decided who gets to look.

**Consent Refused is a different thing.** That toggle in the identity section is not the same as answering No here. Permission No means she does not want her information shared. Her details are still on the record. Consent Refused means she does not want her identifying information in HMIS at all. Turn it on, and the form fills itself in: First name *Generated*, Last name *Refused*, Social Security Number *000-00-0000*, Date of Birth *01/01* with the year left for you, and the codes set to match. On a record that already exists, it deletes what was there first. Use it only when a participant asks that none of their personal information appear in the system.

## Save, Then Say So

**[Text block]**

When you save, the profile opens and the record has a **Unique Identifier**, the eight character code in the header.

LAHSA's own words on what "required" means:

> In order to save the Profile record, you will need to complete all required fields. This doesn't mean that the participant has to answer. It does mean that you need to record what happened in the interaction.

That is the whole lesson in two sentences. Required does not mean answered. It means recorded.

Two clocks worth knowing. Anything you collect goes into HMIS within two business days, and sooner is better. Anything you find out is wrong gets corrected within three.
